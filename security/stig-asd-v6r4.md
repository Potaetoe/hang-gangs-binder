**Open by severity: 1 CAT I, 23 CAT II, 5 CAT III.**

The shape of that table is the assessment's real result. Half the
catalog does not apply — this is not a DoD system, has no passwords, no
SOAP, no SAML, no XML parsing, no CAC/PIV, no ISSO — and of what
remains, the largest single block is not a gap but a **documented
refusal**: 45 requirements are deviations `DESIGN.md` reasoned in
advance, 38 of them in the audit family alone.

That block deserves stating plainly rather than being buried in the
walk, because a STIG review is exactly where it pays off. **A sign-in
log keyed to an account is the membership disclosure this entire design
exists to prevent.** The relevant handles are the few dozen in one
group's member list, so a record of who signed in and when is the oracle
the HMAC'd account id was built to make impossible — and logging IP
addresses and User-Agent strings beside it, as V-222447 and V-222448
require, is the same disclosure in a second form. `DESIGN.md`, "The
identifier is the whole problem", spends most of its length on this and
is where that reasoning lives; the audit family is not 38 things nobody
got round to, it is one decision, taken deliberately, with its reasoning
written down before the requirement was ever read.

One requirement inside that family is *over*-satisfied and worth pulling
out: **V-222444, "the application must not write sensitive data into the
application logs" — Compliant.** There are no `console.*` calls anywhere
in `server/worker.js` or `apps/web/`, and the Worker is specified to see
numeric ids at sign-in and store and log neither.

## Three verdicts that deserve a caveat

Stated because a spot check is a spot check and the honest reading is
more useful than the flattering one:

1. **V-222575/V-222576 (HTTPOnly and Secure cookie flags) are Not
   Applicable, and that is not the same as being safe from what they
   prevent.** There are no cookies; the token lives in `sessionStorage`,
   which is *script-reachable* — precisely what HTTPOnly exists to stop.
   What stands in for it is the CSP: `default-src 'none'` with
   `script-src 'self'`, no `'unsafe-inline'` on any page, and the whole
   policy of every page pinned from outside the page by
   `tools/check_web.py` check 13. That is a strong compensating control
   and it is a different control, so it is worth naming rather than
   letting the N/A imply the risk is absent.
2. **V-222606 (validate all input) is Compliant, with a structural
   limit.** The Worker validates shape and size on every route and
   cannot validate submission *contents* — they are ciphertext by
   design. `DESIGN.md` says so directly ("a member lying, including
   about the handle inside their own sealed blob"), which is why this is
   a stated boundary rather than a gap.
3. **V-222512 (audit who makes configuration changes) is Compliant at
   the version-control level, not the application level.** Every change
   is a commit with an author, and `git log` plus issues is the system of
   record by owner direction. A STIG reviewer expecting an
   in-application configuration audit trail should know that is what
   they are being shown.

## Transport and cryptography

*16 requirements — Open 3 · Accepted Deviation 1 · Compliant 9 · Not Applicable 3*

- **Open** — V-222570–V-222572 (3 requirements) *(CAT II)*
  The algorithms are FIPS-approved - P-256 ECDH, HKDF-SHA256,
  AES-256-GCM, SHA-256, HMAC-SHA256 - but 'FIPS-validated module' is a

  *Evidence:* DESIGN.md 'Threat model' - 'A member lying, including

- **Compliant** — V-222396, V-222397 (2 requirements) *(CAT II)*
  All traffic is HTTPS: Pages enforces HTTPS and the Worker is TLS-only.
  Which TLS versions and ciphers are negotiated is GitHub's and
  Cloudflare's to set - provider side of the line, read from
  configuration and public documentation, not tested here.
  *Evidence:* GitHub API pages config https_enforced=true; apps/web CSP
  connect-src is https-only
- **Compliant** — V-222587, V-222589 (2 requirements) *(CAT I/CAT II)*
  The storage provider holds ciphertext only - a breach, a leaked token
  or a subpoena all yield ciphertext. This is the design's centerpiece
  rather than a control bolted on.

# What would make this a formal pass

This is a reviewed walk against a fetched catalog. It is not a signed
checklist, and the gap between the two is smaller than it looks but not
nothing:

1. **Import the official checklist, not a mirror.** The verdicts above
   are keyed to real V6R4 ids read out of a fetched document, but the
   document was the `cyber.trackr.live` mirror — `stigviewer.com`
   returned 403 and `public.cyber.mil` was not reached directly. A
   formal pass starts from DISA's own `U_ASD_V6R4_STIG.zip` and its
   `.ckl`/XCCDF, which carries what a mirror's summary does not: the
   **Check Text** and **Fix Text** per rule. Several verdicts here are
   judgments about what a one-line title means; against the Check Text
   some of them will move, and the ones most likely to move are the Not
   Applicables in the account-management and audit families, where the
   check often asks "is this documented?" rather than "is this
   implemented?"
2. **Fill in the checklist fields.** A `.ckl` wants a status
   (`NotAFinding` / `Open` / `Not_Applicable` / `Not_Reviewed`), a
   Finding Details and a Comments per rule. This assessment supplies the
   substance for all 286; transcribing it into STIG Viewer is
   mechanical. Note the tool has no "Accepted Deviation" status — those
   45 map to `Open` with the deviation reasoning in Comments, or to a
   POA&M, and **collapsing them to `Open` without carrying the reasoning
   across would be the one way to make this project look worse than it
   is.** The reasoning is the deliverable, not the status.
3. **Close or record the 29 Open items.** Five are filed (#90, #91, #92,
   #93, #95). Seven are recorded as ceremonial or provider-side and
   would be signed off as deviations rather than fixed. Three
   (V-222570–V-222572, FIPS module validation) need a platform
   attestation nobody here can produce, and are the one place a formal
   pass would genuinely stall.
4. **Set a cadence.** V-222515 and V-222624 are about recurrence, not
   the act. A dated reassessment on each STIG revision — V6R4 is six
   revisions in about eighteen months — is what turns this from an event
   into a control. This folder is built for that: a later revision
   arrives as a new file beside this one, and the diff between them is
   the assessment of what changed.
5. **Re-run against a live deployment.** This assessment deliberately
   probed nothing. A formal pass would confirm the response headers, TLS
   parameters and route behavior of the deployed Worker and Pages site
   rather than reasoning from configuration, and `OPERATIONS.md`,
   "Checking a deployment", already has the credential-free probe matrix
   for it.
